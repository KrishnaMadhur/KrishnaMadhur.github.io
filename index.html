F1 · Personal note · October 29, 2026 · 1 min read

A little Mercedes reunion in Shanghai

The 2026 Chinese GP podium felt like a Mercedes family photograph: Kimi, George, Lewis, and Bono. Different overalls now, but plenty of shared history.

The moment that stayed with me was George stepping aside so Kimi, Bono, and Lewis could have their picture together. Lewis’s former engineer, the driver who took his seat, and Lewis himself—there was something lovely about seeing them celebrate together.

As a Mercedes and Lewis fan, this season has given me plenty to enjoy. Ferrari finding more pace, Lewis collecting podiums, and Kimi growing into that Mercedes seat so confidently. Watching his progress makes that Shanghai moment even better in hindsight.

I’ll happily spend a weekend following lap times and strategy calls. Sometimes, though, it’s a small moment after the race that sticks.

First experiences · Personal note · October 29, 2026 · 1 min read

From wheels to blades

The first time I went ice skating was at Penn Ice Rink. I already knew how to roller skate, but ice skating in winter sounded like a fun way to find out how much of that would carry over.

First, I managed to walk through the corridors in the skates without falling. Once I actually stepped onto the ice, it felt surprisingly comfortable. Getting moving was much easier than I’d expected.

Going fast and taking turns were another story. I did fall when I picked up too much speed and couldn’t turn properly, but I enjoyed it enough to go back a bunch of times.

I’m getting better at it. For a first experience, a few falls were a pretty small price for finding something I wanted to keep doing.

A thought · October 29, 2026 · 1 min read

How things come together

There’s something beautiful about familiar ingredients becoming something unexpected. A handful of notes becomes a melody. Simple rules produce intricate patterns. Ordinary words, arranged carefully, say something you’ve never quite managed to express.

The nerdy part of me likes the idea of emergence: interactions giving rise to qualities that the individual pieces don’t have on their own. You can understand the ingredients and still be surprised by what they become together.

Creation feels beautiful for much the same reason. There’s so much possibility in the way things meet, combine, and change one another. Even something small can make the world feel richer than it did before.

And sometimes, that idea becomes quietly personal. You find yourself grateful that something exists, that your paths crossed, and that the world happened to come together in precisely that way.
