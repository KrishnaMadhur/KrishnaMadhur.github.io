CdSe Quantum Dot Synthesis

Singh Center for Nanotechnology · Colloidal Synthesis · UV-Vis · PL · Spring 2026

Synthesized size-tunable CdSe quantum dots via colloidal hot-injection chemistry at the Singh Center for Nanotechnology at the University of Pennsylvania. This project explored the relationship between nanocrystal growth kinetics and the resulting optical properties, demonstrating quantum confinement effects through photoluminescence spectroscopy.

Synthesis

CdSe nanocrystals were synthesized by rapid injection of a selenium precursor (Se dissolved in trioctylphosphine) into a hot cadmium oleate solution at ~250 °C under inert atmosphere. Oleic acid served as the surface-capping ligand, controlling nanocrystal growth rate and preventing aggregation. Aliquots were extracted at timed intervals to capture size-dependent optical evolution.

Characterization

  • UV-Vis Absorption — tracked first excitonic absorption peak shift with growth time, confirming quantum confinement-driven size tunability
  • Photoluminescence (PL) — narrow emission peaks observed across the visible spectrum (green → red) corresponding to nanocrystal diameters of ~2–6 nm
  • TEM Imaging — verified spherical nanocrystal morphology and measured size distribution uniformity
  • Band Gap Extraction — correlated absorption edge to effective band gap using Tauc plot analysis, validating Brus equation predictions
