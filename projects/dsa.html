Directed Self-Assembly (DSA)

Singh Center for Nanotechnology · Block Copolymer · Nanopatterning · Spring 2026

Investigated directed self-assembly of block copolymer thin films for sub-lithographic nanopatterning at the Singh Center for Nanotechnology at the University of Pennsylvania. DSA leverages the thermodynamic microphase separation of block copolymers to generate periodic nanostructures that push well below the resolution limits of conventional optical lithography.

Method

PS-b-PMMA (poly(styrene-block-methyl methacrylate)) thin films were spin-coated onto graphoepitaxial templates pre-patterned with topographic guides. Samples underwent thermal annealing to drive microphase separation into lamellar or cylindrical morphologies, depending on block volume fraction. Selective PMMA removal via O₂ plasma revealed the final PS nanopattern on the substrate.

Results

  • Feature Resolution — achieved sub-20 nm half-pitch periodic line/space patterns via lamellar self-assembly
  • Pattern Ordering — graphoepitaxial confinement guided long-range defect-free alignment across template trenches
  • SEM Verification — top-down imaging confirmed uniform periodicity and pattern transfer fidelity
  • Process Window — mapped anneal temperature and time to pattern quality, identifying optimal conditions for defect-free ordering
