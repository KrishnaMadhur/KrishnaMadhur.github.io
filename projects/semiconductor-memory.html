Robust Compute-in-Memory for Edge Healthcare

ESE 5760 · Semiconductor Memory · Spring 2026

Designed and analysed a robust compute-in-memory (CiM) architecture targeting edge healthcare inference workloads as part of the ESE 5760 (Advanced VLSI) course at the University of Pennsylvania. The project investigates how SRAM-based analog MAC (multiply-accumulate) operations can enable energy-efficient neural network inference directly in memory, eliminating the von Neumann bottleneck.

Architecture

The CiM macro is built around a modified 6T SRAM bitcell array that performs analog dot-product computation on stored weights. Bit-line charge sharing implements multi-bit MAC operations, while peripheral sense amplifiers and ADCs digitize the analog output for downstream processing. Design-for-robustness techniques — including redundancy, ECC, and voltage guardbanding — ensure reliable operation under process variation and low-voltage conditions typical of edge devices.

Key Results

  • Inference Accuracy — maintained classification accuracy within 1–2% of ideal software baseline under realistic process corners
  • Energy Efficiency — analog CiM MAC operations achieved significant energy reduction vs. conventional digital datapath
  • Robustness Analysis — Monte Carlo simulations quantified BER sensitivity to Vt mismatch and supply noise
  • Application Mapping — demonstrated viability for lightweight healthcare classifiers (ECG arrhythmia detection) at the edge
